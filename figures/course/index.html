MRI Physics Bench

Thirteen working instruments for a neuroimaging course, from a hydrogen nucleus to a tractogram. Operate each one, complete its missions, check your understanding and keep a lab notebook as you go. Every moving part runs on the real equation, and most end on the same brain slice.

Operate the instrument

Move the pointer over a figure to take control, or click it and use the keys in its corner. Each one runs a demonstration until you do.

Missions tick themselves

Every lab sets three missions. Reach the state on the instrument and the bench notices; the demonstration alone never counts.

Check yourself

Two quick questions per lab, each with the reasoning behind the answer, and a glossary under Reference.

Notebook and tutor

Keep notes beside each instrument and save them as one file. Where your account allows it, ask Claude about what the instrument shows.

          Tools

          Voxels and millimetres

          An image file stores a grid of voxels, numbered (i, j, k) from one corner. A small matrix saved with it, the affine, turns those indices into millimetres in a shared space, here MNI: x runs towards the subject's right, y forwards and z up, with the origin near the anterior commissure, 14 mm below this slice. Move over the slice, or click it and use the arrow keys, to read both. Then switch how left and right are shown: the same voxel lands on the other side of the screen.

          Diffusion weighting uses δ = 20 ms and Δ = 40 ms, so b = 1000 s/mm² needs a 32 mT/m gradient. The BOLD amplitudes come from the balloon model with the parameters of Friston et al. (2000), whose response peaks earlier than the 4–6 s typical in people; the design and GLM figures use SPM's canonical HRF. Tissue values are approximate; published measurements vary by 10–20% with method and subject. Proton density is relative to CSF. T2* assumes a well-shimmed brain (1/T2* = 1/T2 + 1/160 ms). The IR null is the inversion time that blanks each tissue at TR 9 s. The excess is the fraction of protons in the lower-energy state beyond half, at body temperature (310 K).
